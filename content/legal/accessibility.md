Open Air (operated by Dennis Ramara) is built around accessibility. Accessible colour is the core of the product, and we hold our own interface to the same standard. This statement describes our commitment, the standard we target, known limitations, and how to report barriers.

## 1. Our commitment

We are committed to making the Service usable by the widest possible range of people, including those who rely on assistive technologies. Accessibility is not an afterthought here — the catalog is curated so that colour pairings meet contrast requirements, and the product helps you build accessible interfaces.

## 2. Standard we target

We aim to conform to the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA**. This includes:

- **Colour contrast.** Palettes in the catalog are gated against WCAG AA contrast requirements, and the Studio includes tools to test and repair colour pairings for contrast.
- **Keyboard access.** Interactive elements are reachable and operable by keyboard, and a "skip to content" link is provided.
- **Visible focus.** Focus states are visible for keyboard users.
- **Reduced motion.** We respect the operating-system "reduced motion" preference and minimise non-essential animation accordingly.
- **Semantic structure and labels.** We use semantic markup and accessible labels to support screen readers.
- **Colour-vision support.** The Studio includes colour-vision-deficiency simulation to help you evaluate palettes for users with different forms of colour blindness.

## 3. Accessibility as a feature

Beyond our own interface, the product is designed to help **you** ship accessible work: every palette page documents its contrast pairings, the accessibility centre provides detailed analysis, and the "make any palette accessible" repair tool nudges colours to meet AA contrast while preserving their character.

## 4. Known limitations

Accessibility is an ongoing effort. Some areas may not yet fully conform — for example, certain third-party or embedded components, complex data-visualisation specimens, or newer features may have gaps. We treat accessibility issues as defects and work to fix them.

## 5. Feedback and reporting barriers

If you encounter an accessibility barrier, or need content in an alternative format, please contact us at **dennismmachoene@gmail.com** with a description of the issue, the page or feature involved, and the assistive technology you use. We will respond and work to resolve the issue.

## 6. Ongoing improvement

We periodically review the Service for accessibility and aim to add automated accessibility checks to our development process to protect conformance over time.
